## Summary of the contribution (for a cover letter)

Maps that set the 2016 Leave vote beside the Danelaw or the Civil War are widely shared, and a large literature asks whether historical shocks leave lasting political traces. This paper puts two such claims to a planned test across the 573 constituencies of England and Wales. Seats on the Danelaw side of the line agreed by Alfred and Guthrum voted Leave by 6.9 points more, but with region, density and census composition held fixed the gap is -0.16 points (95% CI -1.81 to 1.49); a boundary design is unstable and finds a jump at a placebo line, while Scandinavian place-names do jump at the real line. Coding the sides taken by the members of the Long Parliament from the 1640 member list, counties with more royalist members show a small negative conditional association with Leave that is not robust to wild cluster bootstrap inference. The resemblance of the maps is a resemblance of places, not, on this evidence, a political memory. Data and code are openly released.
